Term

Kawasaki's theorem

Definition

A single vertex of creases in a flat sheet folds flat exactly when the alternating sum of the angles between consecutive creases is zero — equivalently, when every other angle adds up to 180°. Named after Toshikazu Kawasaki (also credited to Jacques Justin), it is the angle condition that complements Maekawa's count.

Termo

teorema de Kawasaki

Definição

Um vértice isolado de vincos numa folha plana dobra-se em plano exatamente quando a soma alternada dos ângulos entre vincos consecutivos é zero — ou seja, quando os ângulos tomados alternadamente somam 180°. Tem o nome de Toshikazu Kawasaki (também é atribuído a Jacques Justin) e é a condição de ângulos que completa a contagem de Maekawa.

Restricción

Test it exactly when the crease directions allow (rational slopes, exact arithmetic); a floating-point test needs a stated tolerance, or the verdict on a near-miss vertex depends on rounding rather than on the pattern.
